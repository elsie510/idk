The Hidden World of Lakes

What Is a Lake?

A lake is a large body of water surrounded by land. Lakes can contain fresh water or salt water, and they vary greatly in size and depth. Some lakes are small enough to walk around, while others are so large that they resemble oceans. Lakes form in many different ways. Some are created by moving glaciers, while others form inside volcanic craters. Rivers, underground springs and rainfall may supply water to a lake.

Types of Lakes

Glacial Lakes

Glacial lakes form when large sheets of ice move across the land and create deep hollows. When the glaciers melt, water fills these spaces. Many lakes in Canada, New Zealand and northern Europe were formed in this way.

Volcanic Lakes

Volcanic lakes form inside the craters of inactive volcanoes. These lakes are often deep and surrounded by steep rock walls. The water may contain minerals from the surrounding volcanic rock.

Artificial Lakes

Artificial lakes are created by people, usually by building a dam across a river. These lakes are also called reservoirs. They may store drinking water, produce hydroelectricity or provide places for boating and fishing.

Life in and Around Lakes

Lakes provide habitats for many plants and animals. Fish, frogs, insects and freshwater turtles live in or near the water. Birds such as ducks, swans and pelicans depend on lakes for food and shelter. Plants growing around the edge of a lake help prevent soil erosion. Reeds and water lilies also provide hiding places for small animals.

Why Lakes Are Important

Lakes provide water for homes, farms and industries. They are also popular places for swimming, kayaking, fishing and camping. Healthy lakes support biodiversity and help maintain local ecosystems. However, pollution, introduced species and climate change can damage lake environments.

Protecting Lakes

People can help protect lakes by keeping rubbish and chemicals out of waterways. Native plants can be planted along shorelines to reduce erosion and provide wildlife habitats. Visitors should follow local rules, avoid disturbing animals and leave the area as clean as they found it. Protecting lakes ensures that future generations can continue to enjoy them.
